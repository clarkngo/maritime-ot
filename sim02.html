Underneath ECDIS, radar, and every AIS target on a bridge display are lines of plain text: NMEA 0183 sentences, a comma-delimited format that has carried marine instrument data since the 1980s, and the AIVDM sentences that carry encoded AIS traffic inside that same format. This simulator pairs with Lesson 5, where NMEA sentence structure and AIS message content are introduced field by field.

The six sentences below are illustrative examples constructed for teaching — not a live decoder that parses arbitrary pasted input. Each one is paired with a pre-built "Decoded Fields" table so you can see, field by field, what a real receiver would extract from a sentence shaped like it.

What this teaches

Notice that nothing in any of these sentences — not the MMSI, not the reported position, not the navigational status — is cryptographically signed or otherwise authenticated. A receiver decodes exactly what was transmitted and has no way to verify that the transmitting station is who its MMSI claims, or that its reported position matches its true location. This is precisely the gap Lesson 6 builds on: AIS carries no built-in authentication, so nothing stops a transmitted MMSI, position, or navigational status from being false — whether through equipment error or deliberate spoofing.
