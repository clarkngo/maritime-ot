Maritime OT controls physical processes — propulsion, steering, ballast, cargo, cranes — so a mistake made against a live system can injure a crew member, ground a vessel, or shut down a berth. These rules apply to every lesson, lab, and exercise in this course.

Safety outranks the exercise

No activity touches a live bridge, engine-room, ballast, or terminal system. Anything involving physical equipment requires lockout/tagout, PPE, and a qualified supervisor present.

Simulation and isolated equipment only

Labs run on simulators, virtual PLCs, and bench hardware owned for this purpose. Nothing here is aimed at an operating vessel, a live ECDIS, or a working port terminal.

Air-gapped lab segments

Keep lab networks off any production ship or shore network. Verify isolation before every hands-on session, not just once at setup.

Defensive scope

The course teaches architecture, detection, hardening, and response. It does not distribute working exploit code, live malware samples, or attack tooling against navigation or control systems.

Incidents are case studies

Real events (GPS spoofing incidents, port ransomware, vessel cyber incidents) are analyzed for cause, impact, and lesson learned — not reconstructed as a procedure someone could run against a live target.

Protect real-world data

Vessel network diagrams, class-society survey detail, port operator configuration, and any commercially sensitive material stay out of shared work. Synthesize or scrub before anything is published.

Review AI-generated content

Any generated lesson text, quiz item, or graphic gets a subject-matter review and a source check before it is used or shared further. The instructor owns the accuracy.

Accessible and equitable by default

Materials aim for WCAG 2.2 AA and work by keyboard. No lab depends on a paid license or vendor account a learner cannot obtain.

📚 Course Lessons — 12 Lessons / 5 Days
Day 1 · Lesson 1 Foundations
From Isolated Bridges to Converged Fleets
How shipboard control grew up apart from shore IT: mechanical telegraphs and analog autopilots, the arrival of integrated bridge systems and ECDIS, and the satellite links and crew connectivity that finally merged vessel networks with the rest of the world.
Key terms: IBS, ECDIS, Autopilot, Convergence, VSAT →
Day 1 · Lesson 2 Foundations
IT/OT Security Principles at Sea
Why maritime OT inverts the CIA triad, why safety of life at sea sits above all three, and how determinism, availability, and 25-year hull lifecycles break the IT assumptions most learners arrive with. Introduces a vessel/port zone model as a teaching scaffold.
Key terms: SOLAS, Safety, Determinism, Zones & Conduits, ISM Code →
Day 1 · Lesson 3 Foundations
Maritime OT Technologies & Field Devices
A device-by-device tour: ECDIS, radar/ARPA, AIS transponders, GNSS receivers, VDR, engine automation, ballast and cargo control, steering gear, and safety systems. What each one does, how it fails, and what it exposes on the network.
Key terms: ECDIS, AIS, VDR, GNSS, Ballast Control, SIS →
Day 2 · Lesson 4 Protocols
IT Protocols Inside Shipboard & Port Networks
Ethernet, TCP/IP, VLANs, DHCP, DNS, and NTP as they actually appear on a vessel LAN and in a port terminal network — including why satellite backhaul, intermittent connectivity, and GNSS-derived time change how these protocols get used.
Key terms: VLAN, DHCP, NTP, VSAT Backhaul, Segmentation →
Day 2 · Lesson 5 Protocols
Maritime & Industrial Communication Protocols
NMEA 0183 and NMEA 2000, AIS message formats, Modbus and CAN bus in the engine room, and the terminal-side protocols (Modbus TCP, PROFINET, OPC UA) that move a container from ship to yard. Framing at a level you can read in a capture.
Key terms: NMEA 0183/2000, AIS, CAN bus, Modbus, OPC UA →
Day 3 · Lesson 6 Threats
The Maritime OT Threat Landscape
GPS and AIS spoofing/jamming, ransomware crossing from a port's business network into terminal operations, nation-state interest in shipping lanes, and crew/vendor insider risk — taught through documented incidents read as case studies.
⚠ Case study analysis only
Key terms: GPS Spoofing, AIS Spoofing, Ransomware, Insider Risk, Supply Chain →
Day 3 · Lesson 7 Threats
Vulnerabilities & Countermeasures
Legacy ECDIS and radar firmware, flat shipboard networks, default credentials on integrated bridge systems, and the dry-dock-length patch window — then the countermeasures that work anyway: segmentation, jump hosts, allowlisting, and compensating controls.
⚠ No live exploitation
Key terms: CVE, Hardening, Allowlisting, Compensating Control, Patch Window →
Day 4 · Lesson 8 Frameworks
Frameworks for Maritime OT Security
IACS UR E26/E27 cyber resilience requirements, IMO's maritime cyber risk guidelines, NIST SP 800-82, and IEC 62443. How they overlap, where they differ, and how to pick one spine for a course instead of teaching four at once.
Key terms: IACS UR E26/E27, IMO MSC-FAL.1, NIST SP 800-82, IEC 62443 →
Day 4 · Lesson 9 Governance
Governance, Risk & Compliance for Maritime IT/OT
Ship Security Plans, the ISM Code's cyber risk requirements, flag state and port state control expectations, classification society audits, and the asset inventory and risk register that make an audit survivable.
Key terms: ISM Code, ISPS Code, Class Society, Risk Register, Port State Control →
Day 5 · Lesson 10 Identity & Access
Access Control & Identity Management Afloat
Roles for master, officers, engineers, and shore-based vendors; the shared bridge-console problem; privileged remote access for OEM support over a satellite link; and identity lifecycle across a crew that rotates every few months.
Key terms: RBAC, Least Privilege, PAM, MFA, Remote Vendor Access →
Day 5 · Lesson 11 Operations
Monitoring, Detection & Maritime OT Incident Response
Passive asset discovery on a vessel LAN, alarm-log coverage gaps, GNSS-derived time synchronization (and what spoofing does to it), fleet-wide SOC triage over constrained bandwidth, and incident response when you cannot simply unplug a ship mid-voyage.
Key terms: Passive Monitoring, Alarm Log, GNSS Time Sync, Fleet SOC, Containment →
Day 5 · Lesson 12 Capstone
Capstone: Build and Teach Your Own Maritime OT Module
Produce a teachable module — objectives, one hands-on simulation, an assessment with a rubric, and a written safeguards statement — then teach a segment of it and take peer critique.
Key terms: Learning Objectives, Lab Design, Rubric, Peer Review →

These are the four frameworks a maritime OT security role needs fluency in: a design standard purpose-built for ship cyber resilience, an international regulatory guideline that ties cyber risk into the Safety Management System every SOLAS vessel already carries, a general-purpose federal OT risk guide, and a threat-intelligence knowledge base for understanding how ICS incidents actually unfold. Each card opens a dedicated page with the concepts, structure, and classroom framing for that framework.

Design Standard Framework
IACS UR E26 & E27 — Cyber Resilience of Ships
The classification-society unified requirements for cyber resilience: E26 at the whole-ship level (mandatory for new ships contracted from July 2024) and E27 for the security of individual onboard computer-based systems.
Key terms: Newbuild Requirement, Computer-Based System, Security Zones →
Sector Regulation Framework
IMO Maritime Cyber Risk Management — MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3 & Resolution MSC.428(98)
The IMO guideline and resolution that require cyber risk to be addressed inside every SOLAS vessel's ISM Code Safety Management System — the instrument that makes maritime cyber risk an auditable, flag-state matter.
Key terms: ISM Code, SMS, Flag State Audit →
Federal Guide Framework
NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 — Guide to OT Security
The 2023 rewrite that widens ICS guidance to all of OT — including port terminal control systems — tailors SP 800-53 controls into OT-specific overlays, and aligns OT risk management with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework.
Key terms: OT Overlays, NIST CSF, Risk Management →
Threat Intel Framework
MITRE ATT&CK for ICS
A knowledge base of real-world adversary tactics and techniques against control systems — applied here to GPS/AIS spoofing, port ransomware, and documented incidents against shipping and terminal operators.
⚠ Case study analysis only
Key terms: Tactics, Techniques, Detection Engineering →
