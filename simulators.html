About the Simulator Suite

Every simulator in this suite is a self-contained, browser-based page — no install, no account, no paid license, consistent with the accessibility commitments in the course's Safeguards policy. Each one is built around synthetic data generated specifically for teaching, and none of them connect to, or are designed to be adapted to control, a real vessel or port system.

Session-only state

Nothing a learner does inside a simulator is saved between visits — closing or reloading the page resets it, so learners can experiment freely without fear of "breaking" anything.

One simulator, one concept

Each simulator is scoped to reinforce a single lesson's core concept rather than trying to model an entire vessel's systems at once.

Used inside labs, too

Several of the guided labs in Hands-on Lab Exercises are built directly on top of these simulators — this page is the reference for what each one does on its own.

03

Ballast Control Process Simulator

Ballast control is one of the clearest examples of an OT process where a command in software becomes physical consequence — moving water between tanks changes a vessel's trim and stability in real time. This simulator lets a learner operate a simplified ballast control system directly: opening and closing valves, starting and stopping pumps, and watching tank levels and a simplified stability indicator respond.

Pairs with

Lesson 3 — Maritime OT Technologies & Field Devices

Reinforces

The idea that a shipboard control loop has real, physical, sometimes safety-critical consequences — not just a value changing on a screen.

Launch the Ballast Control Process Simulator (sim01.html) →

04

AIS / NMEA Message Analyzer

This simulator loads a synthetic capture of AIS and NMEA 0183 traffic and lets a learner step through it message by message, seeing each sentence decoded into its component fields — position, course, speed, vessel identity, and more.

Pairs with

Lesson 5 — Maritime & Industrial Communication Protocols

Reinforces

Protocol literacy at the field level, and the fact that AIS and NMEA carry no built-in sender authentication — a message is trusted because it arrived, not because it was verified.

Launch the AIS / NMEA Message Analyzer (sim02.html) →

05

Alarm Log Correlation Trainer

Given a synthetic, multi-system alarm log spanning navigation, propulsion, and cargo/ballast alarms, this trainer lets a learner filter, sort, and line up alarms on a shared timeline to practice spotting a plausible cause-and-effect sequence rather than treating each alarm in isolation.

Pairs with

Lesson 11 — Monitoring, Detection & Maritime OT Incident Response

Reinforces

Alarm correlation as a core detection skill, and the reality that meaningful signal often shows up as a pattern across systems, not a single alarm.

Launch the Alarm Log Correlation Trainer (sim03.html) →

06

GNSS Time Sync & Spoofing Simulator

Many shipboard systems derive their time reference from GNSS. This simulator models a simplified GNSS time-sync feed and lets a learner introduce a spoofing event to see how a manipulated time source propagates into dependent systems and logs.

Pairs with

Lesson 6 (threat landscape) and Lesson 11 (monitoring & detection)

Reinforces

Why GNSS-derived time is a dependency worth defending in its own right, not just a positioning concern — a spoofed time source can quietly desynchronize logs across a fleet SOC's evidence trail.

Launch the GNSS Time Sync & Spoofing Simulator (sim04.html) →

07

Access Control Matrix Builder

This simulator gives a learner a blank role-by-system grid and lets them assign an access level to every combination — none, read-only, operate, or administer — for a fictional vessel's crew roles and shore-based vendors.

Pairs with

Lesson 10 — Access Control & Identity Management Afloat

Reinforces

Least-privilege reasoning applied to a realistic shipboard cast of roles, including the shared bridge-console problem and time-boxed remote vendor access.

Launch the Access Control Matrix Builder (sim05.html) →
