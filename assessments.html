01

How Assessment Works in This Course

Every lesson ends the same way: a short, auto-graded quiz that gives immediate, explanatory feedback rather than just a score. The pattern repeats across all 12 lessons so learners always know what to expect, and instructors always know what a given quiz is testing.

Per-lesson quiz

A handful of multiple-choice and scenario-mapping items tied directly to that lesson's key terms and objectives, graded in the browser with an explanation attached to each answer.

Immediate feedback

A learner sees not just whether they got an item right, but why — the explanation restates the relevant concept so a missed question becomes a teaching moment, not a dead end.

No high-stakes gate

Per-lesson quizzes check understanding along the way; they are not designed as a pass/fail gate blocking progress, leaving that judgment to the delivering instructor.

Capstone as culmination

Lesson 12's capstone project is the one rubric-scored, higher-stakes assessment in the course — see Section 04 for the full rubric.

03

Scenario-Based Prompts

Longer-form prompts suited to written response or group discussion, spanning several strands. Each asks the learner to apply a course concept to a fictional situation rather than recall a fact.

Zone Classification Scenario

Given a fictional vessel's system list (ECDIS, autopilot, ballast control, engine automation, crew Wi-Fi), classify each system into the Lesson 2 vessel/port zone model and justify each boundary's protection level.

Framework Selection Scenario

A fleet operator with limited budget asks which single framework from Lesson 8 to adopt first. Using the comparison criteria from that lesson, recommend one and explain what the operator gives up by not adopting the others yet.

Incident Response Scenario

A vessel three days from port shows signs of GPS/AIS spoofing (Lesson 6) mid-voyage. Walk through the Lesson 11 incident response process as it would actually apply without shore support or the option to disconnect.

Access Request Scenario

An OEM vendor requests remote diagnostic access to an ECDIS over a satellite link during a port call. Using Lesson 10's access-control principles, describe the minimum set of controls that would make the request acceptable.

Capstone Rubric

Lesson 12 asks each learner to produce a teachable module of their own — objectives, one hands-on simulation, an assessment with a rubric, and a written safeguards statement — then teach a segment of it and take peer critique. This is the rubric that project is scored against.

Safeguards FramingMissing or vague safeguards statement; defensive-scope boundary not clearly maintained.Includes a clear safeguards statement covering environment and scope, consistent with the course's Safeguards policy.Safeguards statement is specific to the module's own activity, anticipates a plausible misuse, and models the "safety outranks the exercise" principle explicitly.
Activity DesignActivity is passive (reading only) or disconnected from the stated objective.Activity is hands-on or scenario-based and clearly reinforces the stated objective.Activity is well-scaffolded, appropriately paced, and would work for a learner with the course's stated prerequisites with no additional support.

05

Lab Report Template

Every guided lab in this course (see Hands-on Lab Exercises) ends with a short written lab report. This is the section structure that report should follow — a description of what belongs in each part, not a live fillable form.

Objective

One or two sentences stating what the lab was meant to demonstrate or teach — restated in the learner's own words, not copied from the lab page.

Method / Steps

A brief, ordered account of what the learner actually did, referencing the lab's own step outline where it was followed, and noting any deviation.

Observations

What the simulator, tool, or exercise actually showed — data, output, or behavior observed, described factually and separately from interpretation.

Analysis

What the observations mean: how they connect to the lesson's concept, and what a real operator or analyst would take away from the same result.

Safeguards Note

A short confirmation that the exercise stayed within the simulated/isolated environment and defensive scope described in the course's Safeguards policy.

06

LMS Import Notes

This assessment bank is designed to be adaptable to whatever learning management system a delivering institution already uses, rather than tied to one specific platform.

  1. 1

    Multiple-choice items map cleanly to the standard question-bank format most LMS platforms support (a stem, several options, one or more correct answers, and optional per-option feedback).

  2. 2

    Scenario-mapping items — where a learner classifies or matches several elements — often need to be represented as a matching or drag-and-drop item type, or split into a short series of multiple-choice items if the target LMS lacks a matching format.

  3. 3

    Scenario-based prompts (Section 03) and the capstone rubric (Section 04) are best imported as free-response or file-upload assignments with the rubric attached as grading criteria, rather than auto-graded items.

  4. 4

    Preserve the explanatory feedback attached to each quiz item when importing — the immediate-feedback pattern described in Section 01 is a deliberate part of this course's design, not an incidental feature.

This bank is not built against, or claiming certification under, any specific LMS's proprietary import format (QTI, common cartridge, or otherwise) — treat the guidance above as general adaptation notes, and verify the exact import mechanics against your own institution's LMS documentation. Standards alignment referenced in the Quick Facts panel above is informal in the same sense: a description of course structure, not a claim of formal accreditation or certification against an external standard.
